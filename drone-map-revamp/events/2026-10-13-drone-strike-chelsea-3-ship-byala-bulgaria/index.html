Bulgaria reports drone strike on ship CHELSEA 3 off Byala; crew rescued

13 October 2026 · Off Byala · CHELSEA 3 drone strike

Officially reported drone strike and rescue; aerial/surface identification and operator unresolved

Overnight on 13 October 2026 the Maltese-flagged merchant ship CHELSEA 3 was struck by drones in the Black Sea, reportedly 18 nautical miles east of Byala in Varna Province, within Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone and outside its territorial waters. According to the crew’s account, relayed by Bulgaria’s prime minister, three aerial drones hit the deck and hull. A distress notification reached Varna’s maritime rescue centre at 01:30 local time.

The operator and nationality are unresolved. Radev said the evidence was insufficient to accuse either belligerent; he argued that the attacks benefit Russia, but that argument and planned diplomatic consultations do not establish Russian responsibility. The ship is reportedly Ukrainian-owned and registered in Odessa, which is separate from the question of who attacked it.

Border Police evacuated all 15 crew, seven Ukrainians and eight Azerbaijanis, who were brought to Varna; five were treated for initially reported minor injuries, and no deaths were reported. BNR reports that the damaged ship docked at Burgas-West on 17 October; the investigation remains open. On 13 October, another vessel was reported struck and sunk east of Krapets.

MaltaDaily’s account gives 12 October and four minor injuries. Those differences from the Bulgarian account remain unresolved and do not identify a second attack.

On 19 October, Burgas maritime administration director Zhivko Petrov assessed the three hull breaches as blast damage, possibly from a surface drone. This conflicts with the initial aerial-drone account and remains subject to specialist investigation. The vessel was stable in port and a military-prosecutor investigation was underway.

On 20 October, the prime minister reported examination of drone remains and chemical analysis. Chernomore/BGNES specifically reported fragments aboard the vessel and no ammunition or radiation found in initial checks; the BNR/BTA account was less specific about the minister’s recovery statement. Final weapon identification and operator remain unresolved. Chernomore also gives two injured, alongside the earlier four-injured and five-treated accounts; the differing figures are not added together.

On 21 October, the defence minister clarified that recovered material could be from the ship, a drone or another object. NOVA and BNT reported partner teams already inspecting the vessel; fragment identification and explosive analysis were still pending. These developments update the same attack, not a new incident.
