Strike · Finland (later maritime rescue, strike outside Finnish territorial waters)

SPARTA II crew evacuated after reported drone strike in the Gulf of Finland

21 November 2026 · SPARTA II · reported drone strike and Finnish maritime rescue

Reported offshore drone strike; crew evacuated; Russian governor reports arrival at St Petersburg

Finland’s Defence Forces reported a drone strike on 21 November in the eastern Gulf of Finland. The military placed it in international waters, while Border Guard commander Ilja Iljin said it happened in Russian territorial waters. The burning ship later drifted into Finnish territorial waters. That does not establish a drone crossing into Finland.

The Finnish Coast Guard confirmed the crew evacuated to KAPITAN SAKHAROV. At its 22 November briefing the Finnish Border Guard reported one crew member injured. Firefighting continued after visible flames were suppressed; no oil had been observed at sea as of that briefing. That was not a final assessment.

Finnish Defence Forces communications director Aki-Mauri Huhtinen assessed Ukraine was likely responsible. The specific operator, intent and cargo were not independently established.

Estonia’s Foreign Ministry says the damaged vessel drifted from Estonia’s exclusive economic zone into Finland’s exclusive economic zone, then into Finnish territorial waters. Estonia assisted at Finland’s request; the Finnish Border Guard confirmed Estonian participation in firefighting and cooling. The rescue accounts do not establish the strike location or route of a drone.

On 22 November the tow left Finland’s exclusive economic zone and environmental-response area, Finland ended its mission and temporary restrictions were lifted. Helsinki ended precautionary oil-response standby separately. Svenska Yle reported Russia assumed maritime rescue responsibility.

On 23 November Leningrad regional governor Alexander Drozdenko said the crew went ashore at St Petersburg at 20:00 Moscow time. He said the ship reached the same port afloat with its cargo, without giving the ship’s arrival time. His report of no injuries conflicts with the Finnish Border Guard’s report. Final fire and pollution outcomes and the cargo’s identity remain unverified.

Finland’s National Enforcement Authority said it assessed the ship as belonging to an EU-sanctioned company and imposed an interim transfer restriction after it entered Finnish territorial waters on 21 November. The restriction had rescue, safety and environmental exceptions. Final legal disposition was not established.
