GAS HUSKY crew rescued after offshore casualty; drone cause unconfirmed

17 November 2026 · GAS HUSKY · offshore casualty with unconfirmed drone claim

Maritime casualty and rescue confirmed; governor reports four impacts, mechanism unconfirmed

Bulgarian authorities rescued all 19 crew from GAS HUSKY on 17 November 2026 after an offshore maritime casualty. The Liberian-flagged LPG carrier was travelling from Egypt to Romania. The location is in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone, not its territorial waters.

A 10:50 UTC navigation warning described a 45-degree list, submerged bridge and unmanned vessel adrift. It did not establish complete sinking, despite early reports. AP, citing Maritime.bg, reports the rescued captain saying four drones hit the vessel; AP also says there is no official confirmation of the cause. The claim is not independently verified and no operator or connection to BETUL 8 is established.

Manager Stealth Maritime says the vessel was struck early on 17 November in Bulgaria’s EEZ, but does not specify what struck it and says its condition was unknown at the time. Its statement, reproduced by Naftemporiki, confirms LPG loaded at Alexandria for Midia.

A later Maritime.bg report attributes a combined surface- and aerial-drone attack to an unnamed source at the management company. This remains an unconfirmed account; the manager’s public statement does not specify a mechanism.

On 18 November, Varna governor Mario Smurkov told bTV that there had been four impacts, but sailors’ accounts conflicted and drone types remained unconfirmed. He said the vessels had not yet sunk and no pollution had been detected at that time. Four impacts do not establish four independently verified drones.

Reuters reported on 18 November that two unnamed maritime-security sources attributed the strike to an unmanned system. It repeated the governor’s four-impact account without establishing drone type or operator. Reuters described the ship as sunk but provided no newer observation time to resolve the earlier condition reports.

Bulgarian NAVWARN 858/26 gives a later observation at 13:30 UTC on 19 November: the vessel was listing about 45 degrees, bridge submerged, drifting without crew or control. This is not a final-condition or cause determination.

NAVWARN 865/26 reports that at 12:38 UTC on 21 November, GAS HUSKY was still uncrewed and adrift, heavily listing and with its bridge submerged. The observation does not establish a sinking or the original mechanism.

The Philippine Department of Migrant Workers said all 19 Filipino crew were in Varna awaiting repatriation and two injured crew were recovering. Arrival in the Philippines was expected on 26 November, not yet verified. This does not establish the vessel’s final condition.

What was reported
Bulgarian authorities located an overturned vessel presumed to be GAS HUSKY and a nearby life raft. AP, citing Maritime.bg, reports the rescued captain saying four drones hit the vessel, while explicitly noting no official confirmation of the cause. Separate media attribute a drone-strike claim to the Romanian Naval Authority; its original statement was not located. Stealth Maritime, in a statement reproduced by Naftemporiki, says the vessel was struck early on 17 November in Bulgaria’s EEZ, without identifying the mechanism; its condition was unknown to the manager at the time. In an 18:06 report, Maritime.bg cites an unnamed source at the Greece-based management company alleging a combined surface- and aerial-drone attack; this more specific mechanism is not independently confirmed. In a bTV interview published 18 November, Varna governor Mario Smurkov asserted four impacts but described contradictory sailors’ accounts and said the drone types were not confirmed. Reuters’ 18 November dispatch additionally cites two unnamed maritime-security sources attributing the casualty to an unmanned-system strike; the governor reiterates four impacts without identifying drone types.
Aircraft / vessel / quantity
Liberian-flagged LPG carrier GAS HUSKY (IMO 9507764); four drones alleged by its rescued captain in Maritime.bg reporting cited by AP; type unestablished.

Damage / casualties / disruption
All 19 crew rescued. At 10:50 UTC the primary warning described 45-degree listing, submerged bridge and an unmanned drifting vessel. This does not establish complete sinking, despite early reports. BNR reports two sailors to hospital and no pollution recorded at the briefing. In the 18 November morning bTV report, the governor said the vessels had not yet sunk and no pollution had been detected; this is a dated assessment, not a guarantee of later condition. The 18 November Reuters report describes the vessel as sunk, without a newer observation timestamp to reconcile the earlier reports. Bulgarian NAVWARN 858/26 gives a later observation at 13:30 UTC on 19 November: the vessel was listing about 45 degrees, bridge submerged, drifting without crew or control. This is not a final-condition or cause determination. NAVWARN 865/26 reports that at 12:38 UTC on 21 November, GAS HUSKY was still uncrewed and adrift, heavily listing and with its bridge submerged. The observation does not establish a sinking or the original mechanism.
Response / outcome
Bulgarian air and maritime agencies mounted rescue; crew evacuated by helicopter to Chaika naval airbase. Transport ministry says search and rescue ended 13:15 local. Navigation warning remains distinct from rescue completion. The Philippine Department of Migrant Workers reported all 19 Filipino crew were in Varna awaiting repatriation and two injured crew were recovering after treatment. Their arrival in the Philippines was expected on 26 November, not yet verified.
